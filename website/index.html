float labs

Floatblue — GNOME on Bluefin DX, rebuilt with BlueBuild, tracked to Fedora's own release cadence. Hardened, not hostile. Built for the machines everyone else has stopped shipping for. Kifloat (KDE on Aurora) and CoreFloat (headless on uCore) are on the way, in their own repos.

01 Position

Float Labs is an answer to a direction, not a competitor to anybody. The honest summary: a lot of people now feel the modern Atomic-desktop scene has swapped shipping something that works for shipping the newest thing that exists, and that it treats old hardware as a rounding error. Both are defensible engineering calls. Neither is the only defensible call, and there is room left for the opposite bet.

Untested bases are a big bet

Building a daily image on KDE BuildStream, on GNOMEOS nightly, or on the FreeDesktop SDK means shipping whatever landed in that tree this morning. That is a wonderful way to run a demo and a bad way to be somebody's daily driver. We build on the three Fedora-based images that ship a real, staged release: Bluefin, Aurora, uCore.

Driver removal is not "modernity"

Broadcom wl, and a lot of other things that got dropped on the grounds that they are old or unmaintained upstream. For a laptop with a 2013 Broadcom chip that is the difference between a desktop and a network-manager error. Old is not the same as broken, and hardware nobody tests is hardware that still ships to real people.

We keep the Fedora base

No pivot to a non-Fedora toolchain, no private SDK, no build on a nightly. Fedora's own release schedule is the schedule. We bump with Fedora, we soak with Fedora, we inherit Fedora's stability guarantees and we add our own on top.

Hardening that leaves you able to work

sysctls, AIDE, YARA, audit rules, doas, NTS time sync. But sudo stays, Xwayland stays, podman and bootc switch keep working, and no kernel module gets blacklisted that the image itself depends on. Secure and usable, not secure and locked in a cupboard.

Low-end is the design target

3 to 4 GB of RAM, an i5-3550, an iGPU from before Vulkan existed. zram, earlyoom, no file indexer, no shell animations, dirty ratios tuned for a slow disk. This is not a fallback profile. It is the profile everything else is written against.

No money involved

No company, no funding, no sponsors, no paid tiers. This is one person with weaker hardware and a grudge against a design decision, running builds on GitHub Actions. Read CONTRIBUTORS.md: it will stay short.

We are not affiliated with Universal Blue, the Fedora Project, Bluefin, Aurora or uCore. We build on their images and we are grateful for them. Where we disagree, we disagree in our own repos.

02 The three

Three images, three names, three meanings. Same overlay philosophy, same hardening set, same build system, same Fedora cadence — different desktops for different machines. One of them exists today. The other two are on the way, each in its own repo, because a KDE overlay and a headless one are different work with different upstreams.

Floatblue — shipping

GNOME on Bluefin DX. Stock GNOME layout, dash to dock and AppIndicator left exactly as Bluefin ships them because that is how people actually work. Flat Remix theming that follows light and dark across GTK3, GTK4, libadwaita, the shell and the icons. This is the flagship, and it is the successor to the personal image that has been running since before this org existed.

ghcr.io/float-labs-devel/floatlabs · repo

Kifloat — planned

KDE Plasma on Aurora. Plasma 6 on Kinoite, because Aurora is the one place KDE-on-Fedora is actually shipped and staged rather than rebuilt from whatever Plasma landed last week. Breeze and the rest of the KDE stack stay as they are. Same zram, same earlyoom, same hardening, same Unix and BSD toolset.

Its own repo, once there is something to put in it.

CoreFloat — planned

Headless server on uCore. No desktop at all: podman, cockpit, the devops pile, the network toolset, hardened sysctls. The same anti-swap settings the desktops get, and the same care about old NICs and old wi-fi chips, because a server on a ten-year-old box is exactly the case nobody demos.

Its own repo, once there is something to put in it.

What all three share

zram capped at 2 GB, swappiness raised to 180 because the swap is compressed RAM and reluctance just wastes memory. earlyoom instead of the kernel OOM killer, which picks the biggest process and tells you which one. tracker-miners-fs3, PackageKit, man-db and plocate all off. The foundation is defined once, so the three cannot drift apart.

Channels

gts tracks Fedora's ground-to-shoot, the most conservative. stable is the recommended channel: a week of soak before it goes out. latest is the daily canary and the first thing that will break. Each carries the Fedora version it was built against, read out of the image itself.

03 Cadence

Fedora ships roughly every six months and announces it a year ahead. We follow that, not the daily-commit treadmill. Base image pins are tied to Fedora releases; the overlay is rebased on top, tested, then promoted from latest to stable after a soak week. Nothing is promoted on a timer alone — if the smoke test fails, the promotion waits.

On release day

New Fedora version, new base pin, three images rebuilt from scratch on the new toolchain. latest moves, stable does not.

Every day

Overlay commits, rebuilt on latest. This is where breakage is allowed to happen, and where the feedback comes from.

Every week

Soak results reviewed, fixes folded back, latest promoted to stable. ISOs built and published as a release, with checksums.

Every release

Full rebase across all three images, notes written, old release tagged and archived so you can bootc switch back.

Cosign signing on every image, CHECKSUM files on every ISO, and Secure Boot keys you can enrol or ignore.

04 Hardware

This is the section that explains the whole project. The target is a 3 to 4 GB machine with a Sandy Bridge i5 and an iGPU from before Vulkan existed. Every decision above is a consequence of those two facts.

No Vulkan, on purpose

A pre-Haswell iGPU has no Vulkan driver in Mesa. Anything built around it either refuses to start or falls back to software Vulkan and ends up slower than the OpenGL path it replaced. Gamescope and MangoHud are absent for Floatblue. On a Haswell-or-newer GPU, install both — that is the one place performance is deliberately left on the table.

Broadcom and friends stay

Where the RPM exists in Fedora, we keep it and we test it. Old, unmaintained upstream, and the only thing that will ever drive that chip — which is precisely why it needs someone still building it.

Disk gets treated gently

Laptop mode, and smaller dirty ratios so writeback happens sooner and in smaller bursts. An older disk should never be asked to absorb a large buffer and stall while it does.

Games have documented trade-offs

GameMode runs on demand and puts the governor back when the game exits. Two sysctls from the hardening set genuinely block games, and the gaming profile relaxes both: io_uring from 2 to 1 (permission check, not removal — Proton needs it) and mmap_rnd_bits from 32 back to 28 (DXVK's large reservations fail at 32). kexec, BPF, userfaultfd and ASLR itself are never touched.

05 Roadmap

Done

Floatblue as a hardened Bluefin DX overlay, with the low-end profile, the Unix and BSD toolset, doas alongside sudo, Flat Remix theming, blue-team detection tooling, ISOs and a working installer. It has been in daily use, which is the only test that counts.

Next

Splitting the shared foundation out so Kifloat and CoreFloat can compose it instead of forking it, each in its own repo. Then Kifloat on Aurora, bringing the whole Floatblue overlay to Plasma, and CoreFloat on uCore for the headless case.

Then

A hardware compatibility matrix with real machine reports — that is the piece that turns "we kept the Broadcom driver" into something a stranger can check. Per-image bootstrap and upgrade guides. Software we build ourselves, where nothing upstream exists and the gap is worth filling.

Never

Money. Tiers, sponsors, a company, telemetry, or a "professional" edition that is the same thing with a support SLA attached. BlueBuild on stock Fedora bases, tracked to Fedora's own releases.
